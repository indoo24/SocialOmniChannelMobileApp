1. Information We Process

Depending on the features a business enables, we may process information such as:

  • Business account information and identifiers.
  • Meta Business, Facebook Page, Instagram professional account, WhatsApp Business Account (WABA), and phone number identifiers.
  • Names, profile information, user IDs, phone numbers, usernames, and other information made available through authorized platform APIs.
  • Messages, message metadata, attachments, delivery status, timestamps, and conversation history when required to provide inbox and customer-support functionality.
  • Authentication, authorization, webhook, connection-status, and integration data.
  • Technical information such as IP address, browser/device information, logs, error reports, and security events.
  • Information a business or its authorized users enter directly into the service.

1.1 Employee accounts used to access the service Mobile App

Employees authorized by a business to use Scenario (via the mobile app or the web app) have an account with:

  • Email address and password, used to sign in.
  • Name, job title, phone number, avatar, role, and permission set.
  • Availability status (e.g., online/offline).

Employee accounts are created by a business's administrator, not through public self-registration in the app.

1.2 Mobile device and app data Mobile App

When the Scenario mobile app is used, it additionally processes:

  • A push notification token, issued by Firebase Cloud Messaging, and the device platform (Android or iOS), used to deliver notifications.
  • An app-generated installation identifier: a random value the app creates and stores on the device, used only to identify that installation to the push-notification system. It is not a hardware identifier, IMEI, or advertising ID, and it is not derived from any device hardware.
  • Diagnostic data if the app encounters a crash or unexpected error: an error message, stack trace, a short section label, and the screen/route name.

See Section 6, Scenario Mobile Application, for full detail on these mobile-specific flows.

2. How We Use Information

  • To connect authorized business accounts to the OmniChannel application.
  • To receive, display, organize, route, and send customer communications on behalf of the connected business.
  • To provide customer-support, inbox, workflow, reporting, and account-management functionality.
  • To maintain integrations, process webhooks, troubleshoot errors, and protect the service.
  • To comply with applicable law, platform requirements, security obligations, and legitimate legal requests.
  • Mobile App To authenticate employees signing in from the mobile app and maintain their session.
  • Mobile App To deliver real-time updates and push notifications when a new message arrives or a conversation is assigned to an employee.
  • Mobile App To diagnose mobile app crashes and errors and improve app stability.

We do not use this information for advertising, except as described in Section 4, Meta Conversions API, and the mobile app itself contains no advertising or third-party analytics SDK.

3. Meta Platform Data

When a business connects Facebook, Instagram, Messenger, or WhatsApp, Scenario processes data only to provide the features authorized by that business and permitted by the relevant Meta APIs and platform terms. Scenario does not sell Meta platform data.

Access to connected accounts can be removed by the business through its Meta settings or through the Scenario service where such controls are available.

4. Meta Conversions API (Advertising)

Scenario has implemented Meta's Conversions API, an optional advertising feature that is disabled by default for a business's account.

When a business chooses to enable it:

  • Only SHA-256 hashed phone number and/or email address values are sent to Meta — never in plain text.
  • We do not send raw (unhashed) phone numbers or email addresses, IP addresses, internal customer IDs, or browser cookie identifiers to Meta through this feature.
  • The purpose is limited to advertising and ad-delivery optimization for the specific conversion events (e.g., leads, purchases) the business has configured.

A business that has not enabled this feature has no data sent to Meta through the Conversions API.

5. WhatsApp and Messaging Data

If WhatsApp Business Platform functionality is enabled, Scenario may process WhatsApp Business Account identifiers, phone number identifiers, message content and metadata, template information, delivery events, and webhook events needed to operate the connected inbox and messaging features.

The business using Scenario remains responsible for ensuring that its communications, customer permissions, templates, and use of WhatsApp comply with applicable law and WhatsApp/Meta policies.

6. Scenario Mobile Application Mobile App

This section describes, specifically, how the Scenario mobile app (Android and iOS) handles information. It supplements — and does not replace — the rest of this policy.

6.1 Signing in

The mobile app does not offer public self-registration. Employee accounts are provisioned by a business's administrator. Signing in authenticates an employee against their existing account using the same session-cookie authentication used by the Scenario web app — the mobile app does not use a separate or weaker authentication method. A CSRF token is used alongside the session cookie to protect sign-in and other account-changing requests. Passwords are transmitted once, over an encrypted connection, to authenticate the employee, and are never stored on the device.

6.2 API communication with our backend

The app communicates with Scenario's backend over HTTPS for API requests and an encrypted WebSocket connection (WSS) for real-time updates (e.g., new messages). In released versions of the app, this encryption is enforced and cannot be downgraded to an unencrypted connection.

6.3 What is stored locally, on the device

Sensitive values are stored using the operating system's secure, encrypted storage (Keychain on iOS; EncryptedSharedPreferences on Android) — never in plain, unencrypted preferences:

  • The current session identifier, used to keep the employee signed in.
  • The employee's last-used sign-in email, to pre-fill the login field.
  • An app-generated installation identifier (see below).
  • Display preferences: theme (light/dark) and language.

The installation identifier is not a hardware identifier. It is a random value generated on the device by the app itself, unrelated to the device's IMEI, serial number, MAC address, or advertising ID, and it exists only so the backend can address push notifications to this specific app installation. Signing out clears the session identifier and last-used email from the device; the installation identifier, theme, and language remain, since they describe the device/installation rather than the signed-in person.

6.4 Push notifications and Firebase Cloud Messaging

The app uses Firebase Cloud Messaging (a Google service) to deliver push notifications — for example, when a new message arrives or a conversation is assigned to the employee. To do this, the app's push token and device platform (Android/iOS) are shared with Google's Firebase service, solely to route notifications to the correct device. This data is not used by Scenario for analytics or advertising. Google's handling of this data is described at policies.google.com/privacy.

The mobile app does not include Firebase Analytics or Firebase Crashlytics.

6.5 Crash and error reporting

If the app crashes or hits an unexpected error, it sends a short diagnostic report — an error message, stack trace, a short section label, and the screen/route name — directly to Scenario's own backend. This is not sent to any third-party crash-reporting service (no Crashlytics, Sentry, or similar SDK is included in the app).

6.6 Device permissions actually used

On Android, the app requests only two runtime permissions:

  • INTERNET — required for any network communication.
  • POST_NOTIFICATIONS — required to display push notifications.

The app does not request or use camera access, microphone access, contacts access, or location access, on either Android or iOS, and contains no code path that reads device location, the camera, the microphone, or the address book.

6.7 Third-party SDKs in the mobile app

The mobile app's only third-party SDK relevant to this policy is Firebase (Google), used exclusively for push notification delivery as described above. The app does not include any advertising SDK, any third-party analytics SDK, or any third-party crash-reporting SDK.

6.8 Customer data displayed in the app

When an employee opens a conversation, customer record, or order in the mobile app, it displays data that already exists in the business's Scenario account — for example, a customer's name, contact details, messages, notes, and purchase history (see Section 1). This data is not collected from the employee's device; it is retrieved from the backend each time it is viewed, on behalf of the business, in the same way the web app displays it.
